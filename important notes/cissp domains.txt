🔐 CISSP — All 8 Domains
1. 🛡️ Security & Risk Management — 16%

Big question: How does an organization manage security and risk?

Covers:

CIA triad
Security governance
Policies and standards
Risk management
Compliance and laws
Ethics
Business continuity
Disaster recovery concepts
Threat modeling
Security awareness
Supply-chain risk

Think:

"What are we protecting, what could go wrong, and what should the organization do about it?"

This is actually where much of the Module 1 material you're learning now fits.

2. 📦 Asset Security — 10%

Big question: How do we protect the organization's data and assets?

Covers:

Data classification
Asset classification
Data ownership
Data lifecycle
Data retention
Data destruction
Data handling
Data at rest/in transit/in use
Privacy
DLP

Example:

A company has customer PII. Who owns it? Where is it stored? Who can access it? How long should it be retained? How should it be destroyed?

Think:

"Protect the data throughout its entire life."

One important thing for you

CISSP is not an entry-level certification. ISC2 currently requires five years of cumulative full-time experience in at least two CISSP domains for certification, although someone without the experience can pass the exam and become an Associate of ISC2 while gaining the required experience.

So don't worry about studying for CISSP right now.

Your current Google course is giving you the foundation. Then your CS degree, labs, internships, and eventual work experience can gradually take you through these domains.

And honestly, looking at your current interests, you're already touching several of them:

Programming → Domain 8
Networking → Domain 4
SIEM/analyst → Domain 7
Cloud security → Domains 3/4/7
IT/OT → Domain 3
PII/data protection → Domains 1/2
Auditing → Domain 6

So CISSP is basically the big picture of the cybersecurity world you're starting to enter now. 🔐
