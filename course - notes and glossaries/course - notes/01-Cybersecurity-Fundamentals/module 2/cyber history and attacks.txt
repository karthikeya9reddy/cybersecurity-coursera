Cybersecurity History

You learned about incidents that helped shape cybersecurity:

Brain (1986)

One of the early PC viruses.

Lesson: Malware could spread between computers.

Morris Worm (1988)

A worm that spread rapidly across networked systems.

Lesson: Self-spreading malware could cause major network disruption.

ILOVEYOU (2000)

Massive email-based malware attack exploiting human behavior.

Lesson: Social engineering + malware can be extremely powerful.

Equifax (2017)

Attackers exploited a known software vulnerability, leading to a huge exposure of personal information.

Lesson: Patching and vulnerability management are critical.

🎣 Phishing

Phishing = using digital communication to trick people into revealing information or performing a malicious action.

BEC — Business Email Compromise

Attacker impersonates someone in a business context, often to obtain money or sensitive information.

BEC = Business + impersonation

Spear Phishing

Phishing directed at a specific person/group.

Spear = Specific target

Whaling

Spear phishing targeting high-value people, especially executives.

Whale = Big/high-value target

Vishing

Phishing through voice/phone communication.

Vishing = Voice

Smishing

Phishing through SMS/text messages.

Smishing = SMS

Important:

Phishing is a type of social engineering.

🦠 Malware

Malware = malicious software designed to cause harm or perform unauthorized actions.

Malware can steal information, damage systems, disrupt operations, encrypt files, etc.

Virus 🦠

Malicious code that generally requires user execution/action to get started and can infect other files.

Virus = needs execution/user action

Worm 🪱

Malware that can self-replicate and spread between systems without requiring the user to manually open an infected file on each system.

Worm = spreads itself

Ransomware 🔐

Encrypts data and demands payment to restore access.

Ransomware = locks data for money

Spyware 🕵️

Secretly gathers information about users/devices.

Spyware = secretly watches/collects information

Memory trick:

Virus needs you.
Worm spreads itself.
Ransomware locks your data.
Spyware watches you.

🧠 Social Engineering

Social engineering = manipulating humans into revealing information, providing access, or performing an action.

The attacker exploits human trust, emotions, and mistakes rather than relying only on technical attacks.

Social Media Phishing

Attacker researches someone through social media and creates a personalized attack.

Research → personalize → attack

Watering Hole

Attacker compromises a website frequently visited by a particular target group.

Attack the place your targets visit.

USB Baiting

Attacker leaves an attractive/malicious USB where someone will find it and plug it in.

Tempting USB → victim plugs it in → malware

Physical Social Engineering

Attacker impersonates an employee, customer, vendor, etc., to gain unauthorized physical access.

Fake identity → physical access

🎭 Social Engineering Principles

These explain why social engineering works.

1. Authority 👔

"I am your boss/manager."

People tend to respect authority.

2. Intimidation 😨

"Do this or something bad will happen."

Creates fear.

3. Consensus / Social Proof 👥

"Everyone else already did it."

Makes the victim think the action is normal.

4. Scarcity ⏳

"Only one left!" / "This opportunity expires soon."

Creates fear of missing out.

5. Familiarity 🤝

"I know you."

Attacker creates a feeling of familiarity.

6. Trust ❤️

"You can trust me."

Attacker builds a relationship over time.

7. Urgency 🚨

"DO IT NOW!"

Prevents the victim from stopping to think or verify.

⭐ The big picture

Almost everything you've learned so far connects like this:

Threat actor
↓
Uses a technique (phishing/social engineering/malware)
↓
Exploits a vulnerability or human weakness
↓
Creates risk
↓
Security controls reduce the risk
↓
SIEM/monitoring can help detect activity
↓
Analysts investigate and respond
