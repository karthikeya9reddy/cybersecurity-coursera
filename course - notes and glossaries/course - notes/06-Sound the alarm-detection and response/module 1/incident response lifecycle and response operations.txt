1. Incident response fundamentals
Incident response is the process of detecting, investigating, containing and recovering from security incidents.
NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)
The five core functions are:
1. Identify — Understand assets, risks and threats.
2. Protect — Implement safeguards.
3. Detect — Identify potential security incidents.
4. Respond — Take action to manage incidents.
5. Recover — Restore affected systems and operations.
This course focuses mainly on Detect, Respond and Recover.
NIST Incident Response Lifecycle
1. Preparation
Plans, tools, teams and procedures

2. Detection and Analysis
Identify, investigate and understand the incident

3. Containment, Eradication and Recovery
Limit damage, remove the threat and restore operations

4. Post-incident Activity
Document findings and improve future responses

This is a cycle, not a strictly linear process. Phases can overlap as investigators discover new information.

2. Events vs. security incidents
Event: An observable occurrence on a network, system or device.
Security incident: An event that actually or imminently threatens confidentiality, integrity or availability, or violates security policies or applicable requirements.
Remember: All security incidents are events, but not all events are security incidents.
Example:
- A user forgets their password and legitimately resets it → Event.
- An attacker unlawfully takes over the user's account and changes the password → Event and security incident.
CIA triad
- Confidentiality: Information is accessible only to authorized people.
- Integrity: Information remains accurate and is not improperly modified.
- Availability: Systems and information remain accessible when needed.
An incident may threaten one or more of these properties.
3. Investigating incidents: the five Ws
An analyst investigates the following:
Question	What it establishes
Who?	Who triggered or was involved in the incident?
What?	What happened?
When?	When did it occur?
Where?	Where did it occur?
Why?	Why did it happen?
These details help analysts investigate incidents, document evidence and prepare a final report.
Incident handler's journal
A documentation tool used to record and reference incident details throughout an investigation.
Its purpose is to keep findings organized and make it easier to review what happened.
4. CSIRT — Computer Security Incident Response Team
A CSIRT is a specialized group of security professionals trained in incident management and response.
Other names may include:
- Incident Handling Team (IHT)
- Security Incident Response Team (SIRT)
A CSIRT can be a dedicated team or a task force assembled when needed. Its structure depends on the organization.
Command, control and communication
- Command: Leadership and direction for the response.
- Control: Managing technical work, resources and task assignments.
- Communication: Keeping stakeholders informed.
Three key CSIRT roles

1. Security analyst
- Monitors the environment for threats.
- Analyzes and triages alerts.
- Investigates root causes.
- Resolves or escalates alerts.




2. Technical lead
- Manages technical incident response.
- Determines root causes.
- Develops and implements containment, eradication and recovery strategies.
- Coordinates technical work with business priorities.




3. Incident coordinator
- Coordinates across departments.
- Keeps communication clear.
- Ensures relevant personnel receive incident updates.
- Tracks response activities and process compliance.



Other contributors may include HR, legal, IT, management, public relations and communications specialists.
5. SOC — Security Operations Center
A SOC is an organizational unit dedicated to monitoring networks, systems and devices for security threats and attacks.
SOC teams perform defensive, or blue-team, activities such as monitoring, analysis and incident response.
SOC hierarchy
SOC Manager
Hiring, training, performance, reporting and stakeholder communication


Tier 3 — SOC Lead
Team operations, advanced detection and analysis, malware and forensics work


Tier 2 — SOC Analyst
Investigates escalated alerts, refines security tools and reports to the lead


Tier 1 — SOC Analyst
Monitors and prioritizes alerts, manages tickets and escalates issues


Specialized SOC roles
- Forensic investigator: Collects, preserves and analyzes digital evidence to determine what happened.
- Threat hunter: Proactively searches for advanced or previously undetected threats using threat intelligence and analysis.
Note: The exact responsibilities and hierarchy can vary between organizations.
6. Incident response plans
An incident response plan is a document outlining the procedures to follow during each stage of incident response.
Common elements include:
1. Incident response procedures: Step-by-step response instructions.
2. System information: Network diagrams, data-flow diagrams, logging information and asset inventories.
3. Supporting documents: Contact lists, forms and templates.
Testing and improving the plan
Organizations should regularly review and test incident response procedures using exercises such as:
- Tabletop exercises: Team members discuss how they would respond to a simulated incident.
- Simulations: Teams practise responding to a simulated scenario.
These exercises help identify gaps, familiarize team members with procedures and improve response readiness. Some organizations may also be required to conduct exercises for regulatory reasons.
