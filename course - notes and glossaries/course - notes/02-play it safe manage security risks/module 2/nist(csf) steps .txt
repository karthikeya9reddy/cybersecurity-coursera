NIST CSF: A high-level cybersecurity framework organized around Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover to help organizations manage cybersecurity risk.

NIST RMF: A structured 7-step risk-management process — Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, Monitor — used to manage security and privacy risks throughout a system's lifecycle.

🛡️ The 5 NIST CSF Functions

1. IDENTIFY 🔎
"What do we have, and what could go wrong?"
The organization identifies:
- Important assets
- Systems and devices
- Data
- Vulnerabilities
- Threats
- Risks
Analyst example:
You monitor the organization's systems and identify an unusual or vulnerable device.
👉 This is mainly understanding your environment and its risks.
2. PROTECT 🛡️
"How do we prevent or reduce the damage?"
Once risks are understood, security measures are implemented.
This includes:
- Security policies
- Access controls
- MFA
- Encryption
- Employee training
- Security tools
- Procedures
Example:
You discover employees are vulnerable to phishing → the organization introduces security awareness training and stronger email protections.
👉 Protect = prevent/reduce risk.
3. DETECT 🚨
"Is something bad happening?"
You continuously monitor systems to identify potential incidents.
This is where tools you've already learned about become important:
- SIEM
- Logs
- IDS
- Network monitoring
- Alerts
Example:
SIEM
 ↓
Suspicious login detected 🚨
 ↓
Alert security team
 ↓
Investigate

👉 Detect = notice the attack/incident quickly.
4. RESPOND 🚒
"Something happened. What do we do?"
Once an incident is detected, the organization takes action.
This can involve:
- Containing the incident
- Investigating it
- Collecting evidence
- Documenting what happened
- Following playbooks
- Communicating with relevant teams
- Improving security afterward
Example:
Suspicious account detected
        ↓
Investigate
        ↓
Disable/contain account
        ↓
Determine what happened
        ↓
Document incident

👉 Respond = contain and deal with the incident.
5. RECOVER 🔄
"How do we get back to normal?"
After the incident is contained, affected systems and data need to be restored.
This can involve:
- Restoring backups
- Recovering systems
- Restoring data
- Returning services to normal
- Improving recovery plans
Example:
Ransomware encrypts a company's files.
Attack
 ↓
Respond/contain
 ↓
Remove threat
 ↓
Restore clean backups
 ↓
Systems return to normal

👉 Recover = restore normal operations and learn from the incident.

Imagine your house:
IDENTIFY 🔎  
"There's a weak window."

PROTECT 🛡️  
"Install a stronger lock."

DETECT 🚨  
"The alarm detects someone trying to enter."

RESPOND 🚒  
"You follow your emergency procedure."

RECOVER 🔄  
"Repair the damage and improve your security.
