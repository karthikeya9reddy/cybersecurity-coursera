1. Prepare 🧭
"What risks do we need to think about?"
Before an incident happens, identify potential risks and determine what controls might reduce them.
Analyst: Monitor for risks and identify appropriate controls.
2. Categorize 🗂️
"How serious is this asset/data?"
Determine the potential impact on:
- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability
For example, leaking someone's public newsletter subscription isn't as serious as leaking their credit-card information.
3. Select 🛡️
"Which security controls should we use?"
Choose and document controls that will reduce the identified risks.
Examples:
- MFA
- Encryption
- Access controls
- Firewalls
- Security policies
- Monitoring
An analyst might help maintain the relevant playbooks and documentation.
4. Implement ⚙️
"Let's actually put those controls into operation."
The organization implements the selected security and privacy controls.
Example:
Employees frequently have password problems → improve password-management procedures.

This is where the security plan becomes something that actually operates in the environment.
5. Assess 🔍
"Are our controls actually working?"
This is a big one for analysts.
You check whether the controls:
- Were implemented correctly
- Are working as intended
- Have weaknesses
- Need improvement
Example:
Company requires MFA → you check whether MFA is actually enabled for the required accounts.

If something isn't working, you identify it and recommend changes.
6. Authorize ✅
"Is the organization willing to accept the remaining risk?"
Someone with appropriate authority reviews the security/risk information and takes responsibility for the decision to operate the system.
An analyst may support this by:
- Creating reports
- Documenting findings
- Preparing action plans
- Tracking remediation milestones
Important: An entry-level analyst generally isn't the person who makes the final authorization decision.
7. Monitor 👀
"Is everything still secure?"
Security doesn't stop after implementation.
You continuously monitor:
- Systems
- Logs
- Controls
- Threats
- Vulnerabilities
- Changes in the environment
If something changes, the organization may need to reassess its risks and controls.
🧠 Remember it like this
Step	Simple question
Prepare	What risks do we have?
Categorize	How serious are they?
Select	What controls do we need?
Implement	Put the controls in place
Assess	Do they actually work?
Authorize	Accept/approve the remaining risk
Monitor	Keep watching and improve


🔥 One example from start to finish
Imagine a company has a database containing customer personal information.
Prepare: Identify the possibility of unauthorized access.
Categorize: Determine that the data is highly sensitive because it contains PII.
Select: Choose encryption, MFA, access controls, logging, etc.
Implement: Actually configure those controls.
Assess: Test whether unauthorized users can access the database.
Authorize: Management reviews the remaining risk and approves operation.
Monitor: SIEM/logs continuously monitor access for suspicious activity.
And then the cycle continues
