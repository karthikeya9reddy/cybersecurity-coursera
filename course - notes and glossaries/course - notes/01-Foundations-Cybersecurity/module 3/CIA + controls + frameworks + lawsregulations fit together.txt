🔐 Controls, Frameworks & Compliance — Revision
1. The big picture
Think of cybersecurity like this:
CIA → Risk → Framework → Controls → Compliance

CIA Triad
Tells you the security goals:
- 🔒 Confidentiality → prevent unauthorized access
- 📝 Integrity → keep data accurate/trustworthy
- ⚡ Availability → keep systems/data accessible to authorized users
Security Framework
Provides the structured approach for managing risk.
Its four components:
1. Identify/document security goals
2. Set guidelines
3. Implement processes
4. Monitor and communicate results 
Security Controls
Are the specific safeguards used to reduce risks.
Examples:
- MFA
- Encryption
- Access controls
- Firewalls
- Security training
- Backups
Compliance
Means following required internal standards and external regulations/laws.
🧠 Simple distinction:
Framework = plan
Control = protection
Compliance = follow the required rules

🏛️ NIST
NIST = National Institute of Standards and Technology
NIST creates voluntary frameworks and guidelines that organizations can use to manage cybersecurity risk.
Two important ones:
NIST CSF
Cybersecurity Framework
Helps organizations manage cybersecurity risk through:
Govern → Identify → Protect → Detect → Respond → Recover

NIST RMF
Risk Management Framework
Provides a structured process for managing security and privacy risks throughout systems' lifecycles.
🧠 For now:
NIST CSF = cybersecurity risk framework
NIST RMF = structured risk-management process

⚡ FERC-NERC
Relevant to organizations involved with the North American electricity/power grid.
They must protect critical infrastructure and follow Critical Infrastructure Protection (CIP) requirements.
🧠 Remember:
FERC-NERC → Electricity / Power grid ⚡

☁️ FedRAMP
Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program
Designed for the U.S. federal government and cloud services.
It standardizes things such as:
- Security assessment
- Authorization
- Continuous monitoring
- Cloud security
🧠 FedRAMP → Federal government + Cloud ☁️
🛡️ CIS
Center for Internet Security
CIS provides security controls that organizations can use to protect systems and networks.
The important idea:
CIS → practical security controls

🧠 Think:
CIS Controls = "What specific things should we do to defend our systems?"
🇪🇺 GDPR
General Data Protection Regulation
European Union privacy/data-protection regulation.
It protects the personal data and privacy rights of people in the EU.
Organizations need to properly handle personal information and have obligations when certain breaches occur.
🧠 GDPR → Privacy + personal data 🇪🇺
Example:
A company collects someone's personal information.
It needs to have a legitimate reason for processing it and handle that information appropriately.
💳 PCI DSS
Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard
Designed to protect payment-card information.
Applies to organizations that:
- Store
- Process
- Transmit
- Accept
cardholder data.
Goal:
Reduce payment-card fraud and protect card information.

🧠 PCI DSS → Credit/debit cards 💳
🏥 HIPAA
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act
A U.S. federal law protecting patient health information.
Three major areas mentioned here:
1. Privacy
2. Security
3. Breach notification
PHI
Protected Health Information
Health-related information that can be associated with an individual.
Examples:
- Medical information
- Treatment information
- Health insurance information
- Payment information related to healthcare
🧠 HIPAA → Healthcare + patient information 🏥
HITRUST
A security framework/assurance program that can help healthcare organizations address security and compliance requirements.
🧠
HIPAA = law
HITRUST = framework/assurance approach

🌎 ISO
ISO = International Organization for Standardization
ISO develops international standards covering many areas, including technology, security, quality, and management.
For cybersecurity, you'll encounter standards such as ISO/IEC 27001, which focuses on information security management systems.
🧠 ISO → International standards 🌎
📊 SOC 1 & SOC 2
SOC = System and Organization Controls
These are reports used to provide information about an organization's controls.
SOC 1
Primarily focuses on controls relevant to financial reporting.
🧠 SOC 1 → Financial reporting
SOC 2
Focuses on controls related to the Trust Services Criteria, including areas such as:
- Security
- Availability
- Processing integrity
- Confidentiality
- Privacy
🧠 SOC 2 → How well an organization protects information and systems

🔥 The distinction you REALLY want to remember
Don't mix these categories together.
🧠 Models
CIA Triad
→ Describes fundamental security objectives.
🏛️ Frameworks
NIST CSF, NIST RMF, HITRUST, ISO/IEC 27001
→ Structured approaches for managing security/risk.
🛡️ Controls
CIS Controls, MFA, encryption, access control, etc.
→ Specific safeguards that reduce risk.
⚖️ Laws / regulations / standards
GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, FERC/NERC requirements
→ Requirements organizations may need to comply with, depending on their situation.
📑 Assurance reports
SOC 1 / SOC 2
→ Reports that provide information about an organization's controls.
🧠 One final example
Imagine a hospital:
Asset: Patient health information
↓
CIA: Keep it confidential, accurate, and available
↓
Risk: Unauthorized person accesses patient records
↓
Framework: NIST / HITRUST / ISO approach
↓
Controls: MFA + access controls + encryption + logging
↓
Compliance: HIPAA requirements
↓
Monitoring/Audit: Check whether controls are actually working
That's the entire lesson in one scenario.
Asset → Risk → Security objectives → Framework → Controls → Compliance → Monitor
