Course 5 — Security Controls and Data Privacy
1. Security controls
Security controls are safeguards designed to reduce specific security risks. They help protect assets before, during, and after an event.
Three types of security controls
Type	Purpose	Examples from the course
Technical	Uses technology to protect assets	Encryption, authentication systems
Operational	Maintains day-to-day security	Awareness training, incident response
Managerial	Guides how security risks are managed	Policies, standards, procedures
An organization's security policy outlines the controls needed to achieve its security goals.
2. Information privacy and least privilege
Information privacy: Protection from unauthorized access and distribution of data. It also concerns people's ability to decide when, how, and to what extent their personal information is shared.
Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP)
A user receives only the minimum access and authorization necessary to complete their task.
Benefits:
- Limits access to sensitive information.
- Reduces accidental modification, tampering, or data loss.
- Helps reduce the likelihood of successful attacks.
- Supports monitoring and administration.
Example: A customer support employee accesses your payment information only when necessary to assist with your reservation.
Separation of duties
A security principle that divides tasks and responsibilities among different users to prevent one person from having complete control over critical business functions.
3. Data owners and custodians
Role	Responsibility
Data owner	Decides who can access, edit, use, or destroy information.
Data custodian	Responsible for safe handling, transport, and storage of information.
Data steward	Maintains and implements the organization's data governance policies.
Remember: the owner decides how information should be handled; the custodian protects it; the steward helps implement governance rules.
4. User account types
Account type	Purpose
Guest account	Provides access to external users, such as contractors or business partners.
User account	Assigned to staff according to their job duties.
Service account	Allows applications or software to interact with other software.
Privileged account	Has elevated permissions or administrative access.
Every user should generally have their own account, with an appropriate baseline access level.
Auditing user accounts
There are three common approaches:
- Usage audit: Reviews which resources an account accesses and what the user does with them.
- Privilege audit: Checks whether the user's permissions match their current role.
- Account change audit: Reviews account changes for suspicious or unauthorized activity.
Privilege creep: When users accumulate more permissions than they need over time, often because their responsibilities change.

5. Data lifecycle
The data lifecycle describes how data moves through an organization from creation until it is no longer useful.
1

Collect
Gather data from internal or external sources.


2

Store
Keep data in a storage system.


3

Use
Access, process, or work with the data.


4

Archive
Retain data that is no longer actively used.


5

Destroy
Dispose of data when it is no longer needed.



Security controls should protect data throughout every stage, keeping it secure, accessible, and recoverable when needed.
6. Data governance
Data governance is a set of processes that defines how an organization manages information.
Governance policies help ensure data remains:
- Private
- Accurate
- Available
- Secure
A data governance policy outlines how information must be managed, who can access it, and what procedures people must follow.

7. Legally protected information
Term	Meaning	Example
PII	Personally identifiable information that can help identify, contact, or locate an individual.	Name, address, phone number
PHI	Protected health information relating to an individual's physical or mental health or condition.	Patient medical information
SPII	Sensitive personally identifiable information subject to stricter handling and need-to-know access.	Bank account number, login credentials
Key distinction: SPII is a sensitive subset of PII that requires stricter protection.

8. Information security vs. information privacy
Information security (InfoSec)	Information privacy
Protects data in all states from unauthorized users.	Focuses on people's control over their personal information and how it is shared.
Uses safeguards to protect data against threats.	Concerns appropriate collection, use, and disclosure of personal information.
Example: A retailer informs customers how their information will be used and offers an opt-out where applicable. Privacy concerns how the information is collected and shared; security controls help prevent unauthorized access and disclosure.
Both are important for customer trust and brand reputation.

9. Important regulations
Regulation	What it protects or addresses
GDPR	Personal data protection and privacy under EU law. It can apply to organizations outside the EU that handle data of EU residents or citizens, depending on its scope.
PCI DSS	Security standards for protecting payment card transactions and cardholder data.
HIPAA	U.S. law addressing the protection of sensitive patient health information.
Organizations must follow the regulations applicable to their operations, industry, and jurisdiction.
10. Security audits vs. security assessments
Security audit	Security assessment
Reviews controls, policies, and procedures against defined expectations.	Evaluates how resilient current security implementations are against threats.
Checks whether requirements are being met.	Identifies weaknesses and improvement opportunities.
The reading describes audits as typically less frequent, approximately yearly.	The reading describes assessments as more frequent, approximately every 3–6 months.
Example: An audit checks whether all administrator accounts have MFA enabled. An assessment identifies weak passwords and helps determine further security improvements.
Both are part of maintaining security and privacy. The frequencies given are general examples from the course reading, not universal rules.
